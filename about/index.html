Julia Hung (b. 1986, Taipei) creates sculptures and installations exploring transformation, perception, and impermanence. Drawing on techniques traditionally associated with domestic labor—including cooking, ironing, crocheting, and weaving—she transforms discarded plastics, everyday objects, and enameled copper wire into fluid, biomorphic forms. Through these material transformations, Hung examines how systems of value and cultural meaning are constructed, destabilized, and reconfigured across tensions between presence and absence, permanence and ephemerality.

Hung holds a BDes from OCAD University (2012) and an MFA from HEAD, Genève (2016). She was awarded the Asian Cultural Council New York Fellowship (2024) and was a finalist for the New Heads – Fondation BNP Paribas Art Award (2017) and the Taoyuan Fine Arts Exhibition (2023). Her work is held in public and private collections including the King Car Cultural & Educational Foundation, t.Hub Taipei, and Hotel Indigo Alishan.
